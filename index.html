Rebuilt for this page from the notebook's output, using the same eight views as the Tableau workbook. Resolution times cover the 3,775 tickets with a resolution timestamp; CSAT covers the 1,226 rated tickets.

Problem
Support performance can't be judged by ticket volume alone: some tickets get fast responses while others stay unresolved far longer. Where do delays occur, and how do queues and priorities differ?
Dataset
4,000 synthetic support tickets with 10 fields: ticket ID, opened and resolved timestamps, channel, queue, priority, subject, first response time, reopened flag and CSAT score. The data is synthetic and used for analytical demonstration.
Preprocessing
Checked missing values and duplicate ticket IDs (none found), converted timestamps to datetime and calculated resolution time in minutes. 225 tickets have no resolution timestamp, so resolution metrics cover 3,775 tickets. CSAT is missing for 69.35% of tickets.
Method
Descriptive analytics in Pandas: averages and medians by priority and queue, an outlier check on the longest-running tickets, reopen rate and CSAT distribution. A Tableau Public workbook visualizes the same metrics. No machine-learning algorithm is used.
Results
  • Low-priority tickets average 6,544 minutes to resolve, versus 541 for urgent tickets.
  • Integrations (5,732) and Technical (4,982) have the longest average resolution. Medians show the same order (2,560 and 2,351, versus 964 to 1,030 for the other queues).
  • Urgent tickets get a first response in 25 minutes on average. Response and resolution are different stages, so both need monitoring.
  • 285 of 4,000 tickets (7.1%) were reopened.
  • Average CSAT is 3.99 out of 5, but only 30.65% of tickets have a rating.
Evaluation
Descriptive metrics only; there is no predictive model, so no accuracy metrics. Averages are pulled up by extreme tickets (overall mean 3,535 minutes versus median 1,478), so medians were checked alongside them.
Limitations
Synthetic data, so findings would need validating against real support records before operational decisions. Reopened tickets don't prove the original resolutions were poor, and CSAT describes only the rated tickets, not all customers.
Next steps
  • Investigate the Integrations and Technical queues to find what drives long resolution times.
  • Compare resolution times with ticket complexity, escalation history and service-level targets.
  • Break down reopened tickets by queue, priority and channel.
  • Improve survey coverage before using CSAT to judge overall quality.

Top 10 cities by number of restaurants
Problem
Which cities, cuisines and price ranges dominate, and how do price, online delivery and votes relate to customer ratings?
Dataset
Zomato restaurant dataset: 9,551 records and 21 columns covering location, cuisines, cost, delivery, price range, votes and rating.
Preprocessing
Checked structure, data types and missing values. Missing cuisine labels were filled in manually for a handful of restaurants.
Method
Exploratory analysis with Pandas (value counts, group averages, multi-label cuisines split and counted) and Matplotlib charts, plus a Pearson correlation between votes and rating. No machine-learning algorithm is used in this project.
Results
  • New Delhi has the most restaurants (5,473), then Gurgaon (1,118) and Noida (1,080).
  • Average rating rises with price range: 2.00 in range 1 to 3.82 in range 4.
  • Restaurants with online delivery average 3.25, versus 2.47 without.
  • North Indian (3,960), Chinese (2,735) and Fast Food (1,986) are the most common cuisines.
  • Votes and rating are weakly correlated (r ≈ 0.31).
Evaluation
Descriptive statistics and one correlation coefficient. There is no predictive model, so there are no accuracy metrics.
Limitations
The results are associations, not proof that price or delivery cause higher ratings. The data is also heavily concentrated in New Delhi, so findings may not generalize.
